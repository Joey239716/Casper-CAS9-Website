# Brief 02: Cas9 and the guide RNA

You build the hero object: the thing the camera follows for most of the site. It is the Cas9 protein, in clear glass, carrying a single red thread of guide RNA. That red thread is the only colour on screen for most of the story, so it matters enormously.

## Approach: the real structure, simplified

Build the shape from a published crystal or cryo-EM structure of *Streptococcus pyogenes* Cas9 bound to guide RNA and target DNA, downloaded once from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (`https://files.rcsb.org/download/<ID>.pdb` or `.cif`). Candidates to evaluate: **5F9R** (Cas9, sgRNA and double-stranded DNA in the R-loop state), **4UN3** (with a PAM-containing target), **4OO8**. Check what each file actually contains before choosing, and record the id, citation and what you used in your status file; the site's references section will cite it.

Suggested pipeline for `scripts/build-cas9.mjs`:

1. Parse the protein atoms. Splat them into a 3D density grid as Gaussians (a blur radius of roughly 0.6 to 1.0 nm), then extract an isosurface with marching cubes. The aim is a smooth, sculptural, low-resolution form that keeps the real silhouette (the two lobes, the channel between them) and loses atomic bumpiness. It should look like a polished glass sculpture, not a molecular-viewer blob.
2. Smooth and decimate to a sensible triangle count, compute good normals.
3. Use the nucleic-acid atoms in the file to orient it: fit the axis of the DNA/RNA heteroduplex in the central channel, and rotate and translate so that axis is local +Y through the origin (CONTRACT section 4). Convert ångströms to nanometres (divide by 10).
4. Carve the channel: remove protein density within about 1.9 nm of that axis so the site's straight glass helix (2 nm across, opening to a bubble of radius 1.7 nm) passes through without glass intersecting glass.
5. Split the protein into two separately animated meshes, the recognition (REC) lobe and the nuclease (NUC) lobe, by residue range. Verify the ranges against the structure's publication or UniProt Q99ZW2 rather than trusting memory.
6. Extract the guide RNA backbone (phosphorus atoms, in order) as a smooth path for the red thread.
7. Record the positions of the two nuclease active sites, HNH and RuvC, as anchor points.
8. Write a compact output the browser can load quickly (a `.glb`, or a small binary plus JSON; your choice) and keep it under about 1.5 MB.

If, after genuine effort, the real-structure surface cannot be made to look good, fall back to a hand-sculpted stylized form with the same interface, and say clearly in your status file that you did and why.

## Params

| Param | Range | What the viewer sees | Scene |
|---|---|---|---|
| `open` | 0..1 | 1 = the two lobes swung apart on a hinge, clearly empty and waiting. 0 = closed into the compact working shape. | reader |
| `guide` | 0..1 | The red guide RNA thread drawn progressively along its path, threading into the protein. 0 = absent, 1 = fully seated. Its 20-nucleotide spacer end should lie along the channel where it will meet the DNA. | reader |
| `grip` | 0..1 | A slight further tightening around the DNA when the match locks. Subtle. | match |
| `cutPulse` | 0..1 | The two blades act: a brief red flare at the HNH and RuvC sites that peaks mid-range and is gone by 1. | cut |
| `presence` | 0..1 | Overall visibility, for arriving and leaving (scale and opacity together, your judgment). | reader, repair |

Idle motion from `time`: a very slight breathing between the lobes, so it feels alive but never distracts.

## Anchors

`centre`, `hnh`, `ruvc`, `guideTip` (the free end of the spacer), `recLobe`, `nucLobe`.

## Views

`hero` (three-quarter view, whole protein), `close`, `channel` (looking down +Y through the channel), `docked` (as it will be seen sitting on the helix; test with `model=cas9,helix`).

## Acceptance criteria

1. The silhouette is distinctive and asymmetric: recognisably two lobes with a channel, not a sphere or a bean. Someone who knows the structure should recognise it; someone who does not should find it a beautiful object.
2. Smooth, continuous glass surfaces with clean highlights. No marching-cubes stair-stepping, no pinched triangles, no flat-shaded facets.
3. The red thread is elegant: a smooth, even tube that clearly passes *into* the glass and can be seen inside it. It should read as a thread, not a rope.
4. Docked on the helix (`model=cas9,helix` in the lab), the helix passes cleanly through the channel with no visible interpenetration at `helix.unzip=1`.
5. Every param looks good at 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1.
6. Budget: under 3 ms per frame alone at `quality=high`, 8 draw calls or fewer, under 250k triangles. Loads in well under a second.

## Rounds

At least five, and expect most of them to go on the surface quality: blur radius, iso level, smoothing and resolution trade off silhouette against smoothness. Shoot `hero`, `close` and `docked` every round and look at them. Final shots in `agents/shots/02-cas9/final-*.png`: every param at three values, all views, one with `anchors=1`, and docked on the helix.

## The scenes you appear in (for context)

- **reader:** the red guide strand threads into a glass Cas9, which closes around it. From here the camera follows this object.
- **search:** Cas9 hops along the helix from site to site.
- **match:** sitting on the target, the helix opens inside it.
- **cut:** two blades, one for each strand.
- **repair:** it lets go and drifts away.
